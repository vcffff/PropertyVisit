# Grounded directions considered

Buyer scene: comparing homes on a phone between daily commitments and arranging an in-person viewing. Mechanism: property facts connect directly to selectable viewing slots. The opening must make finding and comparing properties easy, not sell the app.

The category rut is a rounded-card feed with a promotional hero; its predictable opposite is an austere editorial poster. Neither is the direction.

1. Residential architecture prospectuses: white and deep blue, system grotesk, price-led photo rows. A familiar residential reference that carries every screen.
2. City wayfinding: white and cobalt, system sans, clear orientation and locality labels. Useful location hierarchy without pretending to provide a map.
3. Residential journals: cool white and graphite, restrained sans, property photography with disciplined captions. Homes lead rather than chrome.
4. Architectural showroom material indexes: mineral blue, navy and white, system type, aligned room/area/price facts. A practical comparison grammar without decorative samples or textures.
5. Civic service directories: pale blue and dark ink, readable system type, explicit actions and states. Trust through clarity rather than badges.
6. Urban atlases: white and petrol, compact sans, place-first grouping and consistent spatial facts. No invented map feature.
7. Relocation folders: white and indigo, system type, clear appointment summaries. Continuity between property selection and visit planning.

Assigned direction: 4. Native navigation and controls remain conventional; the world contributes only type, palette, density, and the repeated comparison-facts signature.

All six catalog challengers lose on audience identification and primary-task clarity when fused with the existing product. Their useful disciplines and explicit verdicts are preserved on the decision board, not their ornamental motifs. Two additional catalog challengers beyond the full-card limit are retained as declined compact options; none is silently omitted.
